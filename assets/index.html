Result 01 · the front door

People are finding you. Most never get through the door.

Hi Sarah,

You scored 78% on the front door — the highest of your four, and the one worth fixing first.

Here is the detail behind that number, what it is likely costing you, and what studios in the same position changed.

The front door leak78%
The second visit problem43%
Scheduling blind spots38%
The revenue ceiling29%
What your answers point to

You told us someone finding you at 9pm has to fill in a form and wait for a reply, and that a first-timer cannot book anything without speaking to you first. Put those together and every new member arrives only if they are patient enough to wait and motivated enough to chase you. That is a high bar for someone who was casually curious twenty minutes ago.

Why it costs more than it looks

1 in 5 members say a complicated booking process on its own would stop them trying a studio. Another 23% say they would not try at all without a low-risk way to try first. These are not people who came, disliked it, and left — they never got far enough to form an opinion. You will never see them in your numbers, which is exactly why this leak stays invisible.

Where it shows up in your week

Engagement on Instagram looks healthy but trial bookings do not move with it. Enquiries arrive as DMs at odd hours and get answered in batches. And a growing share of new faces come through ClassPass or Urban Sports Club and stay there — 37% of members book through those platforms. Once that becomes the default route in, it is both your acquisition channel and your margin.

What changes it

Studios that close this gap almost always change two things at once: they add something low-risk to try, and they make it bookable without a human involved. One without the other does not move much.

"Once a week we offer a $10 community class. It means people try it, and then they see the value."Jane Ellington, Founder, The Shed

Three things you can do this month

  1. Put one low-cost class on the timetable. It does not need to be free — it needs to be small enough that trying costs nothing to decide.
  2. Make that class bookable without a reply from you. The test is whether someone can go from finding you to booked at 9pm on a Sunday.
  3. Give ClassPass arrivals a reason to book direct next time. A first-visit offer that only exists on your own channels is usually enough.
The bigger picture

This is one leak, in one studio. If you know what is stopping people at your door, the next useful thing is knowing what they are weighing up before they get there.

They come once. Most quietly don't come back.

Hi Marco,

You scored 81% on the second visit — the highest of your four, and the most urgent.

Getting people through the door is not your problem. Everything that happens after they walk out is.

The front door leak31%
The second visit problem81%
Scheduling blind spots44%

What your answers point to

You said nothing happens automatically after a first class, and that you find out someone has drifted when they cancel. That combination means the entire decision about whether a first-timer becomes a member is being made in a window you cannot see and do not act in.

Why it costs more than it looks

Over 90% of members decide whether they are coming back within their first three classes, and 23% decide after just one. So the window is not a month — for a quarter of your new faces it closes the same evening. Most studios are still thinking about a follow-up when the decision has already been made.

Where it shows up in your week

Your intake numbers look fine. New people arrive steadily. But membership stays flat, so you are refilling the top every month to stand still, and paying acquisition costs twice for ground you already had. It reads like a marketing problem, which is why studios in your position often spend more on ads and get nowhere.

What changes it

The studios that fix this do not do anything dramatic. They make the follow-up automatic, and they give the first visit a specific reason to become a second — not a discount, a next step.

"With intro offers, around 50% of people convert to subscriptions within two weeks. We have win-back email flows, too."Christina Fell, The Proud Project

Three things you can do this month

  1. Follow up with every first-timer within 48 hours, automatically. If it depends on someone remembering, it will not happen on a busy week.
  2. Give the first visit a defined next step. An intro pack of three or five classes works better than a membership pitch — the commitment matches where they actually are.
  3. Watch for people slipping before they cancel. Someone who came twice a week and has not been in ten days is still winnable. After they cancel, they are not.

Result 03 · the timetable

A waitlist on Thursday. Four people on Tuesday.

Hi Anna,

You scored 86% on scheduling — the highest score we see on this check, and the most expensive one to leave alone.

Some of your classes have a waitlist and some have four people in them. The room and the instructor cost the same either way.

The front door leak34%
The second visit problem41%
Scheduling blind spots86%
The revenue ceiling40%
What your answers point to

You said the timetable is built around instructor availability and has not changed in a while, and that when someone cancels on a Saturday it lands on you. So the schedule reflects who was free when it was written, not when your members actually want to train — and it is rigid, because changing it is manual work you do not have time for.

Why it costs more than it looks

Class times that do not fit is the single biggest barrier in the whole study: 63% of members say it would stop them trying a studio at all. It outranks price, location, and everything else we tested. And it compounds — an empty Tuesday is not just lost revenue that morning, it is an instructor teaching to four people, which is the fastest way to lose a good one.

The part most owners miss

41% of members say better schedules would make them accept higher prices. The same number say it about instructor quality. Scheduling is not only a fill-rate problem — it is the clearest route you have to charging more without anyone objecting.

Where it shows up in your week

Thursday at 7pm is packed and you have had the same conversation about adding a second class for months. Tuesday at 10am has regulars you do not want to abandon and numbers that do not justify it. Both decisions feel like guesses because there is nothing in front of you showing demand against capacity.

Three things you can do this month

  1. Build next term's timetable from booking data, not habit. Look at when people book, when they join waitlists, and when they cancel — the pattern is usually already there.
  2. Set up cover so a cancellation does not reach you. Saturday mornings should not be a group chat.
  3. Check fill rate per class before adding anything. Most studios do not have a scheduling shortage, they have a scheduling mismatch.
The bigger picture

Classes are full. Revenue is flat.

Hi Tom,

You scored 83% on the revenue ceiling, and low on everything else — which is a good problem, and a specific one.

Your studio works. People find you, they come back, the timetable holds up. The constraint is that there is one thing to buy and one price to pay.

The front door leak28%
The second visit problem33%
Scheduling blind spots37%
The revenue ceiling83%
What your answers point to

You said members can buy a class or a membership and not much else, and that you are not sure whether they train anywhere else. That second one usually means yes. When a studio is running well and revenue is still flat, the ceiling is almost never demand — it is the number of ways someone is allowed to spend money with you.

Why it costs more than it looks

78% of members are interested in adding recovery or lifestyle services — massage leads at 59%, sauna at 55%. And 56% already attend more than one studio each quarter; among people who do Pilates, 63% combine it with two or three other activities. That spending is happening now. It is just happening somewhere else, funded by people who already trust you.

The part most owners miss

Referrals are the cheapest growth available to a studio at your stage, and they are mostly untriggered. 34% of members say a free class for both people is the single biggest motivator to recommend a studio — more than discounts, more than anything else. Most studios have never asked.

"Relying on classes alone would limit our growth. By bringing therapists, workshops and different services into one space, we have been able to build a more sustainable business."Keeley Clark, Founder, The Oak House

Three things you can do this month

  1. Add one thing that is not a class. Recovery, a workshop, a guest pass — test it as a pop-up before you commit space to it.
  2. Offer more than one way to commit. Trials, packs, and memberships side by side let people buy at the level they are actually at.
  3. Set up a referral that rewards both people, then actually ask. The asking is the part that gets skipped.
What a conversation would actually cover

You are past the problems most studios are still solving, which makes this worth half an hour rather than another download. We would look at what your members are already spending elsewhere, which of the three moves above fits your space and your team, and what it would realistically add over a season. Your numbers, not a product tour.

Most studios at your stage get this wrong by adding more classes. The ones that break through add more reasons to spend.
